# Cailyn

Cailyn is a native iPhone and iPad personal operations and situational-awareness system.

## Current scope

The current foundation includes the native adaptive application shell, SwiftData domain model, Overview dashboard, Day/Week/Month planning, capacity calculations, actions and waiting workflows, people, operational assets, editable routines, reusable templates, knowledge notes and imported documents, log, local search, and universal capture with a review-before-commit flow. Capture supports typed or on-device spoken input, local rule-based action interpretation, user-approved reminders or prominent alarms, and structured Shift Turnover notes without retaining an audio recording. Holding Capture opens Voice and Shift Turnover shortcuts that begin on-device transcription immediately; voice capture continues through device lock where iOS permits, ignores silence, and finalizes for review at the 30-minute limit or when stopped/interrupted.

Actions, people, assets, routines, templates, knowledge items, log entries, and shift turnovers have direct creation paths and confirmed multi-select deletion where appropriate. Removing or completing an action also cancels its pending Cailyn alert. Selecting a primary tab always returns that tab to its root screen instead of restoring a stale drill-down path. Appearance can follow the system or be locked to a fully adaptive light or dark palette.

Cailyn has dedicated Events and Routines tabs. Events support adding, editing, deleting, and shift filtering; routines can be created, edited, enabled or paused, assigned to a shift, and deleted. Routine checklists support adding, editing, removing, reordering, and completing individual items. Events remain in Cailyn unless Apple Calendar sync is explicitly enabled. Work Shifts default to day 05:00–17:00 and night 17:00–05:00; the boundaries are configurable. Events, log entries, and approved turnover notes automatically inherit the shift for their timestamp, with a day/night override when editing. Routines include optional day/night starter checklists.

Cailyn also includes Voice & Verbal Awareness. It dynamically discovers Apple voices, prioritizes Premium and Enhanced voices installed on the device, supports authorized Personal Voice, and provides priority ordering, critical interruption, duplicate cooldowns, output modes, spoken-category controls, verbal-detail levels, and temporary mute. Voice capture serializes microphone startup, throttles audio-meter and partial-transcript UI updates, and invalidates stale recognition callbacks to avoid overlapping audio taps and UI stalls.

Capture uses a hybrid intelligence pipeline. Deterministic extraction always runs first and remains authoritative for scheduling and saved relationships. On supported OS versions, Apple’s on-device Foundation Model can classify or normalize captured text. Settings offers seven MLX Swift-compatible Hugging Face model choices: Ministral 3 3B, Phi-4 Mini, LFM2 2.6B, Gemma 3 4B, Qwen3 4B, Falcon3 3B, and Qwen2.5 3B. Cailyn keeps at most five downloaded model repositories and loads only one model into memory at a time; users explicitly remove a downloaded model to make room. Models are 4-bit quantized and generation uses a bounded KV cache. The app targets supported physical devices with at least 4,000,000,000 bytes (4 GB) of memory, but larger models may still need more headroom depending on iOS and other app memory use. Model licenses differ, and Settings links to each model card. The Assistant can optionally run the same grounded question through two downloaded models sequentially and display both independent answers side by side; it does not claim agreement is verification or load both models concurrently. Automatic routing prefers the selected loaded local model, then Apple Foundation Models, then deterministic behavior. A downloaded model can also draft event titles/notes and polish log notes; these are drafts only. AI outputs are schema-checked, operational facts are validated locally where applicable, and nothing commits without user approval. Prompts treat captured text as untrusted data and prohibit inventing facts or taking external actions. No Private Cloud Compute or other cloud inference integration is configured.

Knowledge supports user-created nested folders, document moves, and imports of text-based PDF, DOCX, RTF, HTML, TXT, Markdown, and CSV files. Text is extracted on-device, split into overlapping page-aware sections at paragraph and sentence boundaries, and stored in SwiftData with a persistent lexical index and optional on-device English sentence vectors. Image-only PDF pages use on-device accurate OCR (up to 200 pages per document). App Knowledge retrieval fuses BM25 term-frequency/inverse-document-frequency rankings with semantic vector rankings; it falls back to BM25 when Apple Natural Language cannot provide an embedding. This hybrid retrieval follows established [BM25](https://www.elastic.co/docs/reference/elasticsearch/index-settings/similarity) and [reciprocal rank fusion](https://www.elastic.co/docs/reference/elasticsearch/rest-apis/reciprocal-rank-fusion) patterns and is more resilient to paraphrases than the former shared-word count. For a further accuracy improvement, a dedicated cross-encoder reranker and a representative, citation-verified retrieval evaluation set would be appropriate; no such reranker or evaluation corpus is currently bundled. The original file is not copied into Cailyn. Imported text can be searched and removed from the Knowledge tab.

Reminders are local records with date/time, urgency, optional standard notification or prominent alarm, and optional links to routines. They appear in Reminders and, when using a prominent alarm, Alarms; action alerts are included in those views too. Overview surfaces upcoming reminders, and urgency is prioritized before chronological order.

The Assistant retrieves relevant app records and pre-indexed document sections locally, then sends only those excerpts to the selected downloaded model. Answers must use a strict JSON shape with source labels that the app validates against the records retrieved; empty retrieval, unsupported claims marked by the model, malformed output, or invalid citations return “Info not in knowledge base.” instead of displaying unvalidated model text. This makes unsupported answers less likely, but source-cited language-model output is not a guarantee of correctness; users should check cited records for consequential decisions. Document extraction, local search, and answer generation do not upload documents or the app database.

Outlook & Teams integration uses Microsoft Authentication Library and Microsoft Graph with delegated read-only `User.Read`, `Calendars.Read`, and `Mail.Read` scopes. Meetings (including available Teams join links) and a bounded recent email list are read only. Email prioritization is local and configurable by sender/email and keyword; Microsoft Important, flagged/unread status, and urgency terms also contribute. These rules rank and filter messages inside Cailyn; Microsoft consent itself grants mailbox-level read permission and cannot be narrowed to individual senders or subjects. No email, meeting, or account data is written back to Microsoft. Connecting requires a Microsoft Entra app registration, its client ID, the matching redirect URI, and potentially administrator consent; this repository intentionally does not include a client ID.

Calendar items created in Cailyn are also written to a dedicated Cailyn calendar through EventKit after the user grants access. Prominent alarms use AlarmKit and remain app-owned. Knowledge notes can be sent to Apple Notes through the system share sheet because Apple does not offer third-party apps direct Notes database access.

AI planning/replanning, Microsoft account sign-in until Entra registration is configured, CloudKit synchronization, App Intents/Siri/Action Button commands, Spotlight, widgets, the Share extension, and optional biometric app locking are not implemented yet. Model downloads require an internet connection; inference does not.

The app no longer seeds sample operational records. A one-time cleanup removes records matching the previous demo fixtures from existing stores while keeping unrelated user-created records. SwiftUI previews use an empty in-memory store.

On first launch, an initialization screen collects a display name, optional age, and preferred assistant tone (Professional, Warm, Cozy, Direct, or Wild). These preferences stay on-device and can be edited later. App Knowledge mode answers from indexed Cailyn records and cites sources. Conversation mode uses only the local model and recent chat turns, does not search app records or the internet, and has an explicit unknown-answer response. Wild mode permits occasional natural profanity but not language directed at people.

The Experimental Bot Lab separates Persona & Instructions (prompt-only) from local LoRA fine-tuning. Fine-tuning trains a small adapter for the selected downloaded model and stores it separately from the base model; training examples and adapters stay on-device unless the user explicitly exports a JSONL training file. It requires a physical iPhone or iPad reporting at least 8 GB for models up to 3B parameters, or 12 GB for the 4B models, and is unavailable in Simulator. The bounded training run is experimental, not a guarantee of reliable retraining or factual accuracy.

## Generate and build

The checked-in Xcode project is generated from `project.yml` with XcodeGen. Open `Cailyn.xcodeproj` in Xcode. The app targets iOS 18 or newer and supports iPhone and iPad. The Cailyn bundle identifier is `com.austinops97.Cailyn`; iOS treats this as a separate app from Bearing, so it has its own installation and private data store.
